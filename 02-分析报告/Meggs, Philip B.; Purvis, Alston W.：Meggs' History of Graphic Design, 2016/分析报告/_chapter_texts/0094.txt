## centuries. By century’s end, the golden era of chromolithography was coming to a close. One of the most famous lithographic art reproduction firms in the United States, Currier & Ives, produced a variety of sentimental imagery as well as commercial advertising (Fig. 9-61) and went bankrupt shortly after the turn of the century. Changing public tastes and the development of photoengraving were making the use of chromolithography from hand-prepared stones obsolete. The decline was apparent by 1897, when Prang—mindful of the revolution in design sensibilities and technology—merged his firm with Clark Taber & Company, a printing firm specializing in the new photographic-process reproduction of artwork.
