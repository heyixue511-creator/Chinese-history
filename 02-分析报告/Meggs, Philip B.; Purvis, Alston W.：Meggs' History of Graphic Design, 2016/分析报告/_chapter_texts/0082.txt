To be bolder than bold, the compositor used heavier letterforms for the initial letter of important words. Oversized terminal letterforms combine with condensed and extended styles in the phrase Maryland Day! 70 x 34 cm The typographic poster houses that had developed with the advent of wood type began to decline after 1870 as improvements in lithographic printing resulted in more pictorial and colorful posters. Also, the importance of traveling entertainment shows—a mainstay among typographic poster house clients— declined. The growth of magazines and newspapers with space for advertising, and the legislative restrictions on posting, began to shift commercial communications away from posted notices. Although the number of letterpress poster firms declined significantly by the end of the century, wood type continued to be produced until the early 1900s (Fig. 9-20).
