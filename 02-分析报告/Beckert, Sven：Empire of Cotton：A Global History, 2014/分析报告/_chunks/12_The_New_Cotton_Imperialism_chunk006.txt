As the vast expansion of cotton agriculture fed the factories spreading throughout the world, the number of spindles that dotted the global countryside exploded. In 1865, 57 million spindles turned worldwide. By 1920 that number had increased to 155 million.79 Yet increasingly, these spindles and looms did not twist yarn and weave fabrics in the cities and countryside of western Europe and the northern United States, but in those of the global South.
